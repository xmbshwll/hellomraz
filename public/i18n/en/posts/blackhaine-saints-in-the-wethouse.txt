The most interesting rap release of the fall is the debut of Tom Heyes, a choreographer who at one time was noted for his synthesis of the school of Japanese Butoh dance with the aesthetics of the opiumcore aesthetic of Playboy Carti. A singer of the difficulties of the working class in the north of Great Britain, in his full-fledged performing debut, he skillfully deconstructs British rap and island experimental electronica - the result is a bizarre, original noir audio mixture, with simultaneously discernible references to both drill and bass electronics, and at the same time borrowings from gospel and processing in the spirit of Kanye's Yeezus. A most interesting release about people’s internal problems, it sounds like it’s a journey into the depths of one’s own demons.
